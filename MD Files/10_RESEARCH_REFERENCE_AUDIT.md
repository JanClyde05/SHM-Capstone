## Rules for applying these sources

1. Keep historical bridge context dated and attributed. Recheck active restrictions and access rules with the responsible government office.
2. Keep precedent separate from validation. Different sensor, board, mounting, estimator, structure, sampling, and environmental conditions require their own evidence.
3. Describe the firmware output as the “largest in-band FFT-bin frequency” until a validated protocol supports stronger terms such as natural/modal frequency.
4. Do not use a citation to claim a causal bridge-collapse mechanism unless that source reaches that conclusion and its evidence supports it.
5. Before final thesis submission, verify each citation against the exact sentence, source text, and required reference style; repair broken links and resolve uncited entries transparently.

## 2026-10-29 FHWA source recheck - F11 withdrawn

A later exact-phrase search for `10 minute` returned no match on the FHWA 2022 methodologies page because the guide uses `10 minutes` in section 3.8.3. Opening the section directly resolves the point: Step 1 lists hourly, 10-minute, 5-minute, and 1-minute increments for that continuous-count-station AADT calculation, and states that the formula works with any equal-length interval. Chapter 4 of the 2022 guide also allows 5-, 10-, 15-, 20-, 30-, or 60-minute traffic-volume formats and recommends the shortest feasible interval. The original V7 statement that ten minutes is an option in the specified AADT/counting procedure is supported. The statement does not validate the project-specific vibration/count pairing or show that ten minutes is optimal for structural monitoring. A proposed rewrite based on the missed search result was withdrawn, and V7 was restored to the previously verified pre-F11 DOCX/PDF without a net text change.
