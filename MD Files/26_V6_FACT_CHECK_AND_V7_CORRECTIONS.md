## Verified evidence used for the proposed edits

- Sohn, “Effects of environmental and operational variability on structural health monitoring,” PubMed abstract: https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17255051/ . It supports that changing environmental/operational conditions affect measured signals and can mask damage-related changes; it does not say sensor noise changes the bridge’s physical modal frequency.
- Chen et al., “Operational modal analysis of an eleven-span concrete bridge subjected to weak ambient excitations,” publisher page: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0141029616306125 . The abstract describes 46 inexpensive MEMS accelerometers/recorders, PP/FDD/SSI, and noise/weak-excitation effects on modal identification; it supports method context, not this project’s sensor or estimator performance.
- Wang et al. (2022), publisher record: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1002/stc.3073 . It is a review of environmental/operational effects on structural modal-frequency estimates; it does not provide a Buntun-specific threshold.
- Hidalgo-Fort et al. (2024), publisher article: https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/24/15/5078 . The publisher abstract identifies the five authors, two validation/deployment settings, two calibrated commercial comparison systems, and reported error below 1.72% in modal-analysis frequencies. This is a result for that system and its tests only.
- FHWA, 2013 Traffic Monitoring Guide, traffic-monitoring theory: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/tmguide/tmg_2013/traffic-monitoring-theory.cfm . §§1.2 and 1.5 describe manual counts and short-duration traffic monitoring; the guide says typical short-duration counts collect 24-hour, 48-hour, or one-week data.
- FHWA, 2022 Traffic Monitoring Guide, methodologies: https://www.fhwa.dot.gov/policyinformation/tmguide/tmg_2022/traffic-data-methodologies.cfm . §3.8.3 lists hourly, 10-, 5-, or 1-minute data increments for the specified continuous-count-station AADT procedure. This is not a recommendation for a 10-minute bridge vibration session.
- Official component/mounting documentation checked for context: [Espressif ESP32-S3-WROOM-1 datasheet](https://documentation.espressif.com/esp32-s3-wroom-1_wroom-1u_datasheet_en.pdf), [TDK/InvenSense MPU-6000/MPU-6050 specification](https://product.tdk.com/system/files/dam/doc/product/sensor/mortion-inertial/imu/data_sheet/mpu-6000-datasheet1.pdf), and [ISO 5348:2021](https://www.iso.org/standard/78160.html?browse=ics). These support component/mounting facts only, not physical project validation.

## F11 source recheck adjudication - false negative and withdrawn rewrite (2026-10-29)

**Initial finding, withdrawn:** A search for the exact phrase "10 minute" returned no match on the FHWA 2022 methodology page. I incorrectly treated that no-match as evidence that the guide did not list a ten-minute interval, and prepared a replacement paragraph. That inference was wrong; the targeted text search did not capture the guide's plural wording.

**Direct source resolution:** Opening the official FHWA 2022 Traffic Monitoring Guide at section 3.8.3 shows Step 1 explicitly listing hourly, 10-minute, 5-minute, or 1-minute intervals for the continuous-count-station AADT calculation. The guide says the formula works with any equal-length time interval. Chapter 4 of the same guide separately permits 5-, 10-, 15-, 20-, 30-, or 60-minute traffic-volume formats and recommends the shortest feasible count interval. The FHWA 2013 guide describes typical short-duration count stations as collecting 24-hour, 48-hour, or one-week data. These facts support the original V7 wording that the 2022 guide lists 10-minute data as one option for a specific traffic-count procedure.

**Boundary:** This precedent does not establish that a 10-minute concurrent vehicle count is adequate for the study's vibration analysis, nor that it is an optimal interval for the bridge-monitoring question. V7 correctly says its 10-minute count is concurrent context, not an AADT estimate or a standard representative short-duration count.

**Manuscript disposition:** The proposed F11 rewrite was withdrawn. The final V7 DOCX/PDF were restored byte-for-byte from the pre-F11 verified snapshots. No net text change remains from the F11 attempt; the final DOCX still differs from V6 in the same ten paragraphs (F01-F09 and R01). The withdrawn overcorrection and false Unicode-display alarm snapshots are retained under `MD Files/verification/v7_render/` for audit provenance.
