## Findings that should shape the RRL

1. There is a direct, peer-reviewed MPU-6050 precedent: Ribeiro and Lameiras tested a GY-521 breakout using an MPU-6050 in frame and slab vibration experiments. This supports the bounded claim that this sensor family has been used to identify structural vibration frequencies under tested conditions. It does **not** validate this project's assembled PCB, sequential three-device acquisition, selected band/window/peak estimator, mounting, field noise, or accuracy at Buntun.
2. The project's output is currently a largest in-band FFT-bin estimate. An RRL discussion of peak-picking can contextualize that estimator, but it cannot turn a single largest spectral bin into a validated structural natural frequency or a full operational modal analysis (OMA) result. Use “dominant in-band FFT-bin frequency” or “dominant spectral-frequency indicator” until reference testing and an appropriate modal-identification protocol support stronger terminology.
3. A 60-second record is a precedent, not a universal adequacy result. Oth and Picozzi report one-minute PSD windows in their own bridge study. With a 60-second record, nominal FFT-bin spacing is 1/60 Hz (about 0.0167 Hz) only if the *actual* analyzed record spans 60 seconds; a taper such as Hann changes peak shape/resolution trade-offs, and actual per-channel sample timing/count must be measured. The project still needs pilot evidence for its frequency band, sensor, mounting, excitation, estimator, valid-window rule, and repeatability.
4. Bridge modal features vary with traffic and environmental conditions. Literature supports recording and discussing these influences as covariates; it does not justify interpreting the project's observational traffic-group differences as a causal effect of traffic or as evidence of damage.
5. A PCB is part of the mechanical measurement chain. Sensor package orientation, soldered board, enclosure/fixture, attachment to the bridge, stiffness, mass, and mounting repeatability need to be specified and checked. PCB electrical/DRC correctness alone does not establish vibration-measurement fidelity.
6. Traffic-count standards are useful procedure context, but transportation-agency short-count requirements were designed for traffic data programs such as AADT/classification. They do not establish that one-minute observations or five sessions per group are representative or statistically adequate for this bridge-vibration study.
7. Statistical inference needs a declared independent unit, target effect, variance/power rationale, and a plan for nine related channel-axis outcomes. Ten windows within a session are repeated estimates used to summarize that session; they must not silently become ten independent field sessions.
8. No RRL alone can establish the project is “localized,” cheaper in Cagayan, or more maintainable. Those claims need a dated local BOM, supplier/availability evidence, replacement/repair plan, labor and calibration costs, and a fair comparator.

## Source quality and access notes

- **Primary research full text reviewed:** RRL-01 and RRL-10; Lau PDF was available but this pass checked its abstract-level information for relevance. Treat detailed technical claims as pending full-section review where stated.
- **Publisher abstract/metadata or limited full-text access:** RRL-07–RRL-09, RRL-16–RRL-18. An access denial, publisher paywall or search excerpt is not evidence that a claim is false; it does mean the exact source passage has not been verified here.
- **Official technical/manufacturer/statistical guidance:** RRL-02, RRL-11–RRL-15 and RRL-20. Use these for specifications, measurement/statistical procedure and project descriptions within their stated remit, not as peer-reviewed field validation.
- **Standards:** RRL-12 is current per the ISO catalogue (reviewed/confirmed in 2026), but the full standard is paywalled. This log claims only what the official abstract says.
- **Local RRL:** RRL-17–RRL-20 establishes that related Philippine bridge-monitoring work exists. It does not prove that a directly equivalent Buntun study exists or does not exist.
- **Missing:** direct full-text claim audit of every one of the manuscript's 60 references; actual project prototype/reference comparison; current DPWH/Buntun site evidence; Philippine BOM; owner/institution ethics determination; full text review of the selected local conference papers.

## Suggested RRL organization for a later manuscript revision

1. **Vibration-based bridge monitoring and limitations:** explain modal indicators, OMA and environmental/operational variability; distinguish screening indicators from health/safety diagnoses (RRL-06–RRL-10).
2. **Low-cost MEMS evidence:** lead with the direct MPU-6050 study (RRL-01), then comparator systems (RRL-03–RRL-04); state the sensor and system-level limits rather than importing an accuracy number.
3. **Signal processing and window selection:** define the project's largest-bin estimator, Hann window, record duration and nominal bin spacing; contrast with PSD/OMA peak-picking/FDD/SSI and prescribe a pilot/reference check (RRL-05–RRL-06, RRL-10–RRL-11).
4. **Acquisition, mounting and PCB integration:** trace datasheet, actual ODR/filter, sequential sampling, PCB/fixture and bridge mounting; include a validation plan (RRL-02, RRL-12–RRL-13).
5. **Traffic as operational context:** frame the traffic count as an observed proxy, explain interval/counting limitations, and avoid causal interpretation (RRL-08, RRL-10, RRL-14).
6. **Philippine context and bounded gap:** review local bridge/SHM precedents before stating the gap; no “first/only/no prior study” claim without a documented comprehensive search (RRL-17–RRL-20).
